01 · CURRENT PROBLEM

What happens today when supply becomes insufficient?

In a conventional shortage response, a whole feeder or area may be disconnected. The system usually does not know which individual appliance is critical and which one can wait.

1
Demand becomes higher than supplyThere is not enough power to serve every connected load at the same time.
2
A feeder or area is disconnectedUniversity and homes on that line may lose supply together.
3
Critical and flexible loads are treated alikeA server, refrigerator, fan and AC can all be interrupted by the same decision.
Whole-area outage is simple to operate — but it can interrupt far more load than the actual shortage requires.

02 · WHAT THIS CAUSES

The impact is more than “the lights went out.”

🖥️Server & network interruption

Unexpected shutdown can interrupt digital services and risk data or ongoing work.

🎓Classes & lab work stop

Projectors, computers and teaching equipment become unavailable.

🧊Food can spoil

A refrigerator is an essential household load because long outages can damage stored food or medicine.

⚖️Unfair interruption

The same users can suffer repeatedly even when other flexible loads could have been curtailed instead.

This is why the House side in our prototype treats the refrigerator as essential, while fan and AC are flexible.

03 · WHY IPS / GENERATOR IS NOT THE COMPLETE ANSWER

Backup changes the power source. It does not remove the capacity limit.

Example demand8 kW
Generator / reduced grid capacity5 kW

Even after starting a generator, we still need to decide which 5 kW should run.

Battery energy is limitedGenerator capacity is limitedFuel + maintenance cost moneyLarge backup systems are not affordable for everyone
Our controller can manage grid, battery, generator or solar power — because it manages demand, not the source.

04 · OUR SOLUTION

Three smart controllers make one coordinated decision.

The prototype separates the system into a central decision point and two local zones. Each zone knows its loads; the central controller knows the available supply.

SUBSTATION / CENTRALCentral ESP32

Reads available supply, runs the allocation algorithm, updates LCD/buzzer, and sends target states.

UNIVERSITY NODEESP32 + 2 INA219

Monitors university groups and controls C1–C4.

HOUSE NODEESP32 + 2 INA219

Monitors household groups and controls C5–C8.

05 · HOW IT REDUCES LOAD SHEDDING

Reduce the shortage by switching only what is necessary.

The controller continuously compares requested demand with a safe version of the available supply.

Safe capacity= Available supply × 0.973% safety margin in the demo
01No shortage?

If all requested loads fit, every load stays ON. Nothing is shed “just in case.”

02Partial shortage?

The controller turns OFF only enough flexible demand to bring total use back inside the safe capacity — for example, an AC may switch OFF while a server and refrigerator stay ON.

03Supply improves?

Loads are restored in stages as soon as headroom returns, so usable electricity is not left idle.

ConventionalOne switch → whole zone OFF
ProposedSelective curtailment → critical loads continue
